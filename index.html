01 / The constraint

It was never motivation. It was time.

Most operatives don't need convincing that fitness matters. They need a plan that survives a fourteen-hour day, a task that lands late, and a hotel room with no kit in it.

Rosters you don't set

Shifts move. Tasks come in late. A programme built on fixed gym days breaks in the first week.

Long hours, little recovery

Static hours, travel and interrupted sleep take more out of you than the training ever does.

All or nothing

When the only option is a full session, missed days quietly turn into missed months.

02 / Capability

In this job, you are the asset.

Close protection isn't one quality. It's presence, strength, endurance and composure — and the ability to go from standing still to full output with no warning at all.

01

Presence that gets respected

How you carry yourself does work before anything happens. Looking physically capable deters, reassures the client and settles a room — that is part of the job, not a side effect of it.

02

Carry, move, react

Kit, distance and a client to move. Strength and work capacity are job requirements here, not vanity metrics.

03

Long static, sudden maximal

Hours of standing followed by seconds of everything. Your conditioning has to cover both ends of that, not just the middle.

04

Stress and sleep debt

Training done properly buffers fatigue and keeps your judgement sharp late into a shift, when it counts most.

05

Career longevity

Staying capable is how you keep getting selected — this year, and in ten years' time.

03 / The approach

How we give the time back.

Every part of the programme is judged on one question: is this the highest return for the time it costs you?

Short sessions that earn their place

No filler. Every session has a specific job, so nothing has to be made up for later in the week.

Built around the roster

The plan flexes with the week you've actually got, instead of assuming a week you're never going to get.

Nutrition that fits the shift pattern

A personalised plan built for petrol stations, hotel breakfasts and meals eaten at odd hours — not for someone with a kitchen and a quiet evening.

Accountability without another meeting

Weekly check-ins that fit into your day rather than a fixed slot you'll end up cancelling anyway.

04 / Who it's for

Built for operatives, not general population.

This is for you if

  • You work in close protection — employed, freelance, residential or on rotation.
  • You're roughly 25 to 40 and the job has quietly taken over your training.
  • You want to be physically capable for the role, and carry the presence that goes with it.
  • Your time is limited and you're honest about how limited it is.
  • You'll do the work, as long as someone gives you the right work to do.

Probably not for you if

  • You're chasing a stage-ready physique above everything else.
  • You want a generic plan you'll never adjust or report back on.
  • You're not willing to train consistently, even in short sessions.
  • You'd rather have a quick fix than something that holds for years.
